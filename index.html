      System Comparison: Standard Base-6 vs. Bijective Base-6

      Decimal Standard Senary (Digits 0-5) Bijective Base-6 (Digits 1-6) Key Structural Difference
      1 1 1 Identical representation
      5 5 5 Identical representation
      6 10 (rolls over early) 6 (single digit!) In bijective, 6 is a legitimate single digit
      7 11 (1×6 + 1) 11 (1×6 + 1) First 2-digit bijective number
      12 20 16 No zeros permitted in bijective: 1×6 + 6 = 12
      36 100 56 5×6 + 6 = 36 (compact without leading zeros)
      42 110 66 6×6 + 6 = 42 (all maximum digits before rollover)
      43 111 111 First 3-digit bijective number (1×36 + 1×6 + 1)

      How to Perform Addition

      Addition is performed column by column, from right to left. The key difference from decimal is how you handle carrying over when a sum exceeds 6.

      How to Perform Subtraction

      Subtraction also works column by column. Borrowing is where it gets interesting: when you borrow from a column, you are borrowing a full 6, not 10.

      How to Perform Multiplication

      Multiplication follows the same long-multiplication algorithm as decimal, but it uses the bijective base-6 multiplication table. Each partial product is calculated and then summed using bijective addition rules.
